# FFY2022 MAS Portfolio Summary

## Overview

The FFY2022 ISWS CHAMP grant portfolio consists of **12 documents** covering **11 distinct projects** (ISWS 22-10A is an amendment to 22-10, not a new award). Together they represent approximately **$3.5M in FEMA funding** across program management, community outreach, statewide LOMR processing, county-wide DFIRM update completions, and multiple data development phases on major Illinois river systems. All FRP MAS projects share the same Period of Performance (09/01/2022 – 09/30/2025), while the PM and COMS SOWs run only through 09/30/2023. FEMA Region 5 Regional Project Officer is John Wethington for all awards; Glenn Heistand is PM on all projects except the LOMR (Christopher Hanstad).

The portfolio spans two distinct categories:
1. **Program support** (PM, COMS, LOMR) — $487,500 combined, covering statewide administration and community engagement.
2. **Flood Risk Projects (FRP)** — approximately $3.02M combined, covering a spectrum from early-phase scoping/survey through final DFIRM production, across geographies ranging from Chicago suburbs to rural southern Illinois.

## Projects

### ISWS-22-LOMR: Statewide LOMR/CLOMR Review
- **Activity Type:** Letter of Map Revision (LOMR) / Conditional LOMR (CLOMR) Review — CTP assumes FEMA's review and recommendation authority for all Illinois LOMR/CLOMR case submissions statewide.
- **Phase:** Ongoing recurring program (not a multi-year study toward a new FIRM). Each grant year is a stand-alone performance cycle.
- **Scope:** ISWS receives and processes CLOMR/LOMR requests from across Illinois, performing initial review, technical review, preparation of draft determination documents, QA/QC, coordination with FEMA for final issuance, and post-processing. Cases involving levee review are excluded and forwarded to FEMA's contractor. Budget is based on an estimated **30 applications at $9,250 each**. Cases must be processed within 60 days of a complete submittal (30 days faster than the 90-day statutory deadline). PMR cases that grow out of LOMR reviews are referred to separate MAS awards.
- **Deliverables:** Completed CLOMR/LOMR case packages, draft determination documents, audit submissions, distribution of determination letters; quarterly case-count progress reports.
- **Period:** 09/01/2022 – 09/30/2024
- **Budget:** $277,500 ($9,250/case × ~30 estimated cases; no CTP leverage)
- **Multi-year:** No — this is a recurring annual-cycle program. Each MAS is self-contained.
- **Recommended Next Steps:** Renew LOMR review MAS for FFY2023. Track actual vs. estimated case volume; if case load has grown, consider requesting budget adjustment in the next award. Confirm PMR spin-outs (e.g., Poplar/Spring Creek — see 22-04/22-05) have separate MAS funding in place before FIRM issuance.

Includes production of Flood Risk Products alongside the regulatory DFIRM panels.
- **Deliverables:** Preliminary FIRM panels and FIS report, preliminary Flood Risk Products dataset, community meeting materials, due process letters, final effective FIRM panel set and FIS.
- **Period:** 09/01/2022 – 09/30/2025
- **Budget:** $295,000 (no leverage)
- **Multi-year:** Yes — this MAS is the final phase of a multi-year project. Prior MASs funded data development (hydrology, hydraulics, floodplain mapping) in Bond County. This grant year brings the project to final effective FIRM status.
- **Recommended Next Steps:** Complete preliminary map production and QA/QC per MIP milestones. Schedule and conduct community coordination meetings prior to Preliminary Map Delivery. Manage the 90-day due process / appeal period. Resolve any technical appeals and issue the effective FIRM. Archive final deliverables in MIP.

### ISWS-22-07: Embarras River — Hydrology Analysis and Channel/Bridge Survey
- **Activity Type:** Flood Risk Project (FRP) — Data Development (Survey Data Capture + Hydrology Data Capture)
- **Phase:** Phase 2 of a multi-phase Embarras River countywide DFIRM program — **Survey and Hydrology**. Survey and hydrology data are intermediate steps; hydraulics, floodplain mapping, preliminary, and effective FIRM production all remain for future phases.
- **Scope:** Embarras River mainstem, **198.36 riverine miles**, spanning 8 counties (Lawrence, Crawford, Richland, Jasper, Cumberland, Coles, Douglas, Champaign) and major communities including Lawrenceville, Newton, Charleston, and Villa Grove. Work includes: (1) hydrology analysis for the full Embarras mainstem using Bulletin 17C stream gage analysis + USGS regression equations; (2) field survey acquisition for channel cross-sections and approximately all bridges and culverts along the mainstem. A survey contractor is to be procured (TBD at time of award). ISWS performs QA/QC of survey data and hydrology. Project Outreach and Communication Plan (Task 3) includes risk communication outreach to the 8 counties.
- **Deliverables:** Hydrology Data Capture package (HEC-HMS or equivalent model, frequency analysis, gage analysis), Survey Data Capture package (photos, sketches, cross-sections, bridge/culvert as-built data, spatial files), Project Outreach and Communication Plan; all submitted to MIP.
- **Period:** 09/01/2022 – 09/30/2025
- **Budget:** $775,000 (survey contractor TBD; no leverage)
- **Multi-year:** Yes — hydrology and survey are precursor steps only. This MAS explicitly does not include hydraulics, floodplain mapping, or preliminary map production. Multiple future MASs will be needed: hydraulic data capture, floodplain mapping/DFIRM development, preliminary production, due process, and effective FIRM.
- **Recommended Next Steps:** Procure survey contractor via competitive solicitation. Complete hydrology analysis and submit to MIP for FEMA review. Begin hydraulic data capture scoping (future MAS). Plan for multi-county coordination given 8 separate FIRM panels will ultimately need to be updated; consider phasing hydraulic work by HUC-8 sub-watershed.

### ISWS-22-08: Kaskaskia River — Pre-Data Development (USACE Model Review + 2D Watershed Modeling)
- **Activity Type:** Flood Risk Project (FRP) — Pre-Data Development (General Data Capture)
- **Phase:** Phase 1 (earliest phase) — **Pre-Data Development / Scoping**. No hydrologic, hydraulic, or mapping deliverables are produced; this phase establishes the study scope and methodology for future data development projects.
- **Scope:** Upper, Middle, and Lower Kaskaskia HUC-8 watersheds, focusing on the counties of **Fayette, Shelby, and Washington** (unmodernized) plus coordination on the mainstem Kaskaskia River (which has an existing USACE model). Two distinct objectives:
  1. **USACE Kaskaskia Model Review** — Perform a comprehensive review of the USACE's existing Kaskaskia River model and develop a plan (in coordination with USACE and IDNR-OWR) to repurpose it to FEMA regulatory standards.
  2. **2D Watershed Modeling for Fayette/Shelby/Washington** — Develop 2D watershed-scale models at the HUC-10 level using General Data Capture MIP tasks. Models serve as a scoping tool to determine study needs and methods for future data development projects in these three un-modernized counties. Anticipated deliverables: 2D watershed models, proposed study lines, identification of non-levee features, and a recommendations report. Many 2D models are expected to carry forward into future data development with minimal enhancement.
- **Deliverables:** USACE model review report and enhancement plan; 2D watershed models for Fayette/Shelby/Washington County HUC-10 subwatersheds; proposed study lines; non-levee features identification; recommendations report for future data development.
- **Period:** 09/01/2022 – 09/30/2025
- **Budget:** $899,500 (largest single FRP award in the portfolio; no leverage)
- **Multi-year:** Yes — explicitly a scoping/pre-data development phase. Multiple future MASs are required: full data development (hydrology, hydraulics, survey) for Fayette/Shelby/Washington Counties, plus a separate USACE Kaskaskia mainstem hydraulic update MAS.
- **Recommended Next Steps:** Coordinate USACE model review with USACE Rock Island/St. Louis districts and IDNR-OWR early in grant period to avoid scheduling delays. Deliver 2D watershed models and recommendations report to inform FFY2023 or FFY2024 data development MAS proposals. Use this scoping work to develop accurate cost estimates for subsequent data development MASs in Fayette, Shelby, and Washington Counties.

## Cross-Cutting Observations

### 1. Portfolio Spans All Mapping Phases Simultaneously
This FFY2022 portfolio reflects a well-distributed workload across the full FIRM lifecycle:
- **Scoping/Pre-Data:** Kaskaskia (22-08)
- **Survey-only:** Kishwaukee (22-09), Embarras survey component (22-07)
- **Hydrology/Data Development:** Embarras (22-07), White County (22-11)
- **Post-Data / Preliminary through Effective:** Bond County (22-02), Richland County (22-03), Cook/Kane PMRs (22-04/22-05)
- **Program support:** PM (22-01), COMS (22-10/10A), LOMR (22-LOMR)

This spread is healthy but also means Glenn Heistand is PM on 10 of the 11 projects simultaneously.

### 2. Multiple Projects Are Early Phases Requiring Substantial Future Investment
Three of the seven FRP MASs are explicitly early-phase:
- **Kishwaukee (22-09):** Survey only → needs hydrology, hydraulics, floodplain mapping, preliminary, and effective FIRM (4–5 future MASs)
- **Embarras (22-07):** Survey + hydrology → needs hydraulics, floodplain mapping, preliminary, and effective FIRM (3–4 future MASs)
- **Kaskaskia (22-08):** Pre-data scoping → needs full data development for Fayette/Shelby/Washington Counties + USACE mainstem update (3–5 future MASs per county)

These represent significant future grant commitments and should be tracked in the CNMS.

### 3. Two Projects Converging Toward Effective FIRM in This Cycle
Bond County (22-02) and Richland County (22-03) are both in their final phases (QR2 through Effective), and Cook/Kane County (22-04/22-05) PMRs are also in their final due-process phase. These should be prioritized for community meetings, due process management, and effective date coordination with FEMA.

### 4. Poplar/Spring Creek PMR Split Across Two Counties
The Cook County (22-04) and Kane County (22-05) PMRs are companion projects covering the same Poplar/Spring Creek watershed. They should be tracked together and their effective FIRM dates synchronized. The smaller Kane County budget ($58K vs. $125K) likely reflects a smaller geographic share of the watershed.

### 5. Kaskaskia Pre-Data is the Largest Single Award at $899,500
The scope is ambitious: USACE model review plus 2D watershed modeling for three counties. Given the scale and multi-agency coordination required (USACE, IDNR-OWR, 40+ communities), this project carries the highest schedule risk in the portfolio and should receive active monitoring.

### 6. White County Is Closing Out a Multi-Year County Study
At only $53,700, White County (22-11) represents the last piece of a multi-year countywide effort. A small preliminary-through-effective map production MAS should be queued for a future grant year to complete the countywide DFIRM.

### 7. COMS Amendment (22-10A) Signed After Performance Period
The amendment to the COMS SOW was signed on 05/28/2025, roughly 20 months after the original 09/30/2023 performance end date. This should be clarified with FEMA Region 5 to ensure the administrative record is correct. The funding total was unchanged.
