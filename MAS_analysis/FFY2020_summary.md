# FFY2020 MAS Portfolio Summary

## Overview

The FFY2020 CHAMP portfolio contains **14 documents** covering 13 flood risk project MASs plus 1 Program Management SOW. The portfolio spans three broad categories: (1) **completion/post-preliminary processing** for counties already through data development (Clay, Effingham, Cook/Kane); (2) **new Data Development** studies building H&H models to KDP-2 but explicitly requiring separate future MAS grants before DFIRM production (Bureau, Johnson, Massac, Pope, Wayne-Skillet, Kishwaukee, and supplemental work for Peoria); and (3) **targeted continuation/engagement** projects resolving specific technical blockers before a study can advance (Rock River – East Moline levee, Turtle Creek – bi-state hydraulic model). A statewide LOMR Review Partners MAS rounds out the portfolio.

All documents are dated June 2020 and reference the CTP Partnership Agreement of September 9, 2013. The Cooperative Agreement period for FY20 grants is not explicitly stated in most MASs (they defer to Award Notice terms); only the LOMR MAS specifies an end date of **June 30, 2022**.

### ISWS_20-06: Turtle Creek — Resolution of Bi-State H&H and Mapping

- **Activity Type:** Hydraulic Data Development (bi-state H&H model resolution) + FRR Meeting
- **Phase:** Technical resolution phase — Study has been ongoing since ISWS15-04 (FFY2015) and ISWS17-05 (FFY2017). This MAS resolves outstanding bi-state modeling disputes before workmaps and FRR can proceed.
- **Scope:** A WDNR 2012 study covered a 1-mile IL reach of Turtle Creek near the IL/WI state line. ISWS15-04 updated the IL reach model; ISWS17-05 attempted to incorporate Fehr Graham's newer community-submitted model but revealed significant upstream impacts extending ~3 miles into Wisconsin. A Nov. 7, 2019 FEMA/ISWS/IDNR-OWR/WDNR meeting identified multiple outstanding issues (railroad embankment treatment, zero-rise vs. floodway-standard differences between states, cross-section truncation). This MAS tasks ISWS to develop a single hydraulic model acceptable to both states, prepare draft work maps spanning IL and WI, resolve floodway, and hold a Flood Risk Review meeting with all stakeholders.
- **Deliverables:** Resolved bi-state hydraulic model; floodway analysis (single model or two-state models); draft work maps; FRR meeting convened; post-FRR documentation; Illinois and Wisconsin regulatory submittals
- **Period:** Per Cooperative Agreement Award Notice (FFY2020; June 2020 MAS date)
- **Budget:** $144,100 (FEMA)
- **Multi-year:** Yes — 3rd generation (ISWS15-04 → ISWS17-05 → ISWS20-06). After model resolution and FRR, a future MAS will be needed to produce and issue Preliminary FIRM panels and complete post-preliminary processing for both states.
- **Recommended Next Steps:** Complete bi-state hydraulic model; hold FRR meeting with IL and WI stakeholders; after model acceptance by IDNR-OWR and WDNR, scope a Preliminary/PPP MAS for both states' FIRM panels

## Cross-Cutting Observations

### 1. Pipeline Structure: Three Distinct Tiers

The FY20 portfolio divides cleanly into tiers:
- **Tier 1 – Completion** (Clay, Effingham, Cook/Kane): All three are late-stage, executing QR3 through Final Maps. These should produce effective FIRMs within the FY20–22 performance period.
- **Tier 2 – Mid-stream / Technical Blockers** (Rock River, Turtle Creek, Peoria): Studies have substantial prior investment but face specific unresolved technical or coordination issues before they can advance. Peoria is closest to completion (targeting QR3); Rock River and Turtle Creek need FRR before preliminary.
- **Tier 3 – Early Data Development** (Bureau, Johnson, Massac, Pope, Wayne-Skillet, Kishwaukee): All end at KDP-2. None will produce a DFIRM under this grant; all require at least one additional MAS cycle for DFIRM production. **This represents the majority of the portfolio budget (~$4.24M of $5.71M).**

### 2. Long-Running Projects Dominate

Many projects trace back many years:
- **Peoria**: 5 MAS generations since FFY2011 (ISWS11-16 → 12-05 → 13-05 → 18-04 → 20-13)
- **Rock River**: Started with a 2014 FEMA study, then ISWS17-06, now ISWS20-05
- **Turtle Creek**: ISWS15-04 → ISWS17-05 → ISWS20-06
- **Clay & Effingham**: Both trace to ISWS17-03

### 3. Southern Illinois Concentration

Three new Data Development projects (Johnson, Massac, Pope) are all in southern Illinois counties along the Ohio River. All three involve Ohio River redelineation, datum conversion (NGVD29→NAVD88), coordination with the Kentucky Division of Water, and major levee system considerations (Reevesville, Brookport, Golconda). These projects would benefit from coordinated scheduling and shared boundary condition data. None of these counties have had prior Discovery, and each will require a future DFIRM production MAS.

### 4. Kishwaukee as Largest Investment / Longest Horizon

At $1.37M, Kishwaukee (ISWS_20-12) is the single largest project in the FY20 portfolio and is explicitly described as the first of at least four separate MAS grants. This is a 10–15 year commitment to bring the entire Kishwaukee HUC8 to updated DFIRM standards. Discovery (MAS17-02) has been completed; Phase 1 initiates H&H for Winnebago County. Planning for Phases 2–4 (Boone County, McHenry County, tributary HUC-10s) should begin concurrently with Phase 1 execution.

### 5. Bureau County — Potential Green River Watershed Coordination

The Bureau County study (ISWS_20-07) includes Green River reaches in four counties (Bureau, Henry, Whiteside, Lee) because of model tie-in requirements. ISWS notes this will "facilitate future mapping updates in the Green HUC8 watershed." A future HUC8-level MAS for the Green River watershed may be warranted, building on the Bureau County H&H investment.

### 6. Levees as a Recurring Risk Factor

Nearly every project in the portfolio identifies levees as a significant risk factor or scope driver: Tiskilwa Levee (Bureau), Karnak/Cache River Levee (Johnson), Brookport/Reevesville Levees (Massac), Golconda/Reevesville Levees (Pope), multiple levees in Wayne County (Skillet), Defenbaugh/Taylor-Chapman/Lonza/Evonik Levees (Peoria), East Moline levees (Rock River), and Turtle Creek railroad embankment (treated as de facto levee). In nearly all cases, levee LAMP outreach and hydraulic significance analysis are deferred to FEMA/RSC (STARR II), which creates schedule risk for downstream DFIRM production.

### 7. Future MAS Requirements — Budget Planning Signal

At minimum, the following future MAS grants will be needed to complete the FY20 projects:
- Bureau County: 1 DFIRM production MAS (QR3 → Final)
- Johnson County: 1 DFIRM production MAS
- Massac County: 1 DFIRM production MAS
- Pope County: 1 DFIRM production MAS
- Wayne County (combined Skillet + Little Wabash): 1 combined DFIRM production MAS
- Kishwaukee HUC8: 3+ additional data development MASs + 1 DFIRM production MAS
- Peoria County: 1 Preliminary/PPP completion MAS
- Rock River: 1–3 PMR production MASs (after FRR)
- Turtle Creek: 1 Preliminary/PPP MAS for both IL and WI panels
