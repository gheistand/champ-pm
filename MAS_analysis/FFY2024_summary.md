# FFY2024 MAS Portfolio Summary

## Overview

The FFY2024 portfolio contains **10 base MAS/SOW agreements** plus **8 scope amendments** (designated "A" suffixes), for 18 total documents. The 10 base agreements represent: 1 Program Management (PM) SOW, 1 Community Outreach and Mitigation Strategies (COMS) SOW, and 8 Flood Risk Project (FRP) Mapping Activity Statements covering distinct geographic areas across Illinois. All agreements run under the Illinois State Water Survey (ISWS) Cooperating Technical Partners (CTP) program, FEMA Region 5, with Glenn Heistand as CTP Project Manager.

**Total FEMA Funding (base MAS/SOWs): ~$3,241,000**

The 8 "A" amendments (24-02A through 24-10A) are administrative scope modifications with no new funding — all identically remove references to the Climate and Economic Justice Screening Tool (CEJST) and equity-related indices (Social Vulnerability Index, National Risk Index, Community Disaster Resiliency Zones) from community assessment and stakeholder engagement tasks. These were signed by Glenn Heistand on 05/28/2025, with FEMA countersignatures pending as of that date.

The FRP portfolio spans a wide range of project maturity: two projects are in active **data development** (pre-Preliminary), two are in **Preliminary map production** through Open House, one is in **QR1–QR3 Preliminary production** (not yet distributing), and three are in **Post-Preliminary Processing** (the final phase before effective maps). Period of performance for all projects: **10/1/2024 – 9/30/2027**.

## Cross-Cutting Observations

### 1. Portfolio Maturity Ladder
The FFY2024 FRP portfolio spans all stages of the FIRM production lifecycle simultaneously:

| Stage | Projects |
|---|---|
| **Data Development** (pre-Preliminary) | 24-08 (Kishwaukee Phase 5), 24-10 (Embarras Tributaries) |
| **Preliminary Production (QR1–QR3 only)** | 24-09 (Macoupin) |
| **Preliminary Production + Open House** | 24-06 (Johnson), 24-07 (Bureau) |
| **Post-Preliminary / Final** (terminal MAS) | 24-03 (Peoria), 24-04 (McHenry Nippersink), 24-05 (McDonough) |
| **PM/COMS (support)** | 24-01 (PM), 24-02 (COMS) |

Three projects (24-03, 24-04, 24-05) are the closest to final effective FIRM maps — all are in Post-Preliminary Processing (Due Process through LFD). These should be prioritized for close coordination during the appeal period.

### 2. Large Data Development Investment
Nearly **$1.95 million (60%)** of the $3.24M FRP budget is in data development (24-08 Kishwaukee at $963K and 24-10 Embarras at $986K). These projects will not produce FIRMs by themselves — each will require 2–3 additional MASs (Preliminary, Post-Preliminary) before reaching effective maps. The Embarras study alone covers 7 counties and 1,049 miles of streams, making it the most geographically extensive project in the portfolio.

### 3. Pipeline Gap — Future MAS Needs Are Explicit
Five of the eight FRP projects explicitly defer major workflow phases to future (currently unfunded) MASs:
- **24-06 (Johnson):** Needs Post-Preliminary MAS for Due Process, Final Map Production, LFD
- **24-07 (Bureau):** Needs Post-Preliminary MAS for Due Process, Final Map Production, LFD
- **24-09 (Macoupin):** Needs a Preliminary Distribution MAS (Task 23) *and* a Post-Preliminary MAS
- **24-08 (Kishwaukee):** Needs Draft FIRM/Preliminary MAS *and* Post-Preliminary MAS for 3 counties (DeKalb, Kane, McHenry)
- **24-10 (Embarras):** Needs multiple Preliminary and Post-Preliminary MASs for up to 7 counties

This represents a significant forward-funding requirement that should be captured in future FFY Business Plans.

### 4. Recurring Watersheds and Multi-Phase History
- **Kishwaukee River basin** appears in both 24-02 (COMS stakeholder engagement) and 24-08 (Phase 5 data development), reflecting a long-running multi-phase study effort.
- **Spoon watershed** appears in both Peoria County (24-03) and McDonough County (24-05), reflecting the shared hydrology of central Illinois.
- Several projects reference engineering from MASs stretching back to 2011 (ISWS 11-16 for Peoria) and 2015 (ISWS 15-01 for McHenry), illustrating that effective FIRMs in complex counties may require 10–15 years of cumulative MAS work.

### 5. Blanket CEJST Amendment — All A-Suffix Documents
All 8 "A" amendments (24-02A through 24-10A) are **identical in structure and purpose**: removing Climate and Economic Justice Screening Tool (CEJST) and related equity-index language (SVI, NRI, CDRZ) from Sections 2.3.3 and 2.4.1 of each MAS. These were signed by Glenn Heistand on 05/28/2025. As of that date, **all 8 still awaited FEMA Region 5 countersignature** (John Wethington's date field was blank in every document). These amendments should be tracked for full execution.

### 6. Levee Risk Is Pervasive
Levee coordination is a named schedule risk in six of eight FRP projects:
- **24-03 (Peoria):** 10+ levee systems in NLD with varied accreditation status
- **24-04 (McHenry):** No specific levees named but risk flag present
- **24-05 (McDonough):** Levee inventory needs verification (table appears templated)
- **24-06 (Johnson):** Two non-accredited levees (Cache River/Karnak, Reevesville) mapped as Natural Valley
- **24-07 (Bureau):** Two Tiskilwa levees with expired PAL status
- **24-10 (Embarras):** Eight non-accredited levees with mapping path TBD

Early and continuous engagement with the FEMA Region 5 RSC Levee Lead is critical across the portfolio.
