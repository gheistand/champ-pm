# FFY2021 MAS Portfolio Summary

## Overview

The FFY2021 ISWS CHAMP portfolio consists of **13 Mapping Activity Statements (MASs)** totaling approximately **$3.62 million in FEMA funding**. The portfolio spans the full spectrum of Risk MAP activities: statewide program management (PM), community outreach and mitigation strategies (COMS), LOMR review, and 9 Flood Risk Projects (FRPs) across Illinois. The FRPs break into three broad categories: (1) near-final PMR/redelineation projects carrying existing county-level studies through database development to QR1 (Rock Island, Whiteside, Henry), (2) a project completing a Paper Inventory Reduction to effective (Stark), and (3) multi-phase data development projects for counties and watersheds where new H&H analysis is needed to produce new FIRM panels (Montgomery, Hamilton, Wabash, Bond, Kishwaukee). Many projects are explicit continuations of prior-year MASs, reflecting the multi-year lifecycle of DFIRM production. No project in this portfolio completes the full lifecycle to effective FIRM panels in FY21 alone—all FRPs will require at least one additional future MAS for Preliminary or Post-Preliminary Map Production.

All projects list **Glenn Heistand** as CTP Project Manager and **John Wethington** as FEMA Regional Project Officer. The LOMR project uses **Christopher R. Hanstad** as PM. Period of Performance for FRPs is 09/01/2021 – 09/30/2025; PM and COMS SOWs run 09/01/2021 – 09/30/2023.

### ISWS-21-LOMR: LOMR Review Partners — Statewide Illinois
- **Activity Type:** LOMR Review Partners (CLOMR/LOMR Technical Review)
- **Phase:** Ongoing operational program; FY21 is a standalone 2-year grant
- **Scope:** ISWS assumes responsibility for reviewing and recommending approval of all CLOMR and LOMR requests across the entire State of Illinois. The CTP performs initial and final technical review, prepares draft determination documents, conducts QA/QC, handles fee administration, post-processes issued LOMRs, manages appeals and comment processing, and maintains MIP records. FEMA retains authority for final issuance. Levee-related cases are referred to FEMA's contractor. Key staff include Chris Hanstad (PM/Engineer), Brian Chaille, Jimmy Powell, Addison Jobe, Shelly Fuller (post-processing), Sam Chakravorty, and Greta Buckley (GIS).
- **Deliverables:** MIP case entries within 24 hours of receipt; acknowledgment letters; draft CLOMR/LOMR determination documents; QA/QC review documentation; post-processing database submittals; audit-ready case files.
- **Period:** August 1, 2021 – September 30, 2023
- **Budget:** $370,000
- **Multi-year:** No (distinct operational program; each grant year is independently scoped). Program is ongoing across grant cycles.
- **Recommended Next Steps:** Continue LOMR review program with a follow-on FY23 MAS. Monitor for potential Physical Map Revision (PMR) triggers from significant LOMRs.

- **Phase:** Phase 1 (first MAS for Montgomery County); new study initiation
- **Scope:** Performs full H&H data development for Montgomery County streams. No Discovery, no Flood Risk Products, no mapping delineation table entries—data development deliverables go to QA/QC and then presumably a future FIRM database and preliminary map MAS.
- **Deliverables:** H&H reports (hydrology and hydraulics) with independent QA/QC review notes; topographic dataset; base map; project management documentation; communication plan; PICC meeting materials.
- **Period:** 09/01/2021 – 09/30/2025
- **Budget:** $400,000
- **Multi-year:** Yes. This is Phase 1 / Data Development only. Future MASs will be required for: floodplain mapping/delineation, Draft FIRM Database, Preliminary Map Production, and Post-Preliminary Map Production.
- **Recommended Next Steps:** After H&H QA/QC is complete, fund a continuation MAS for floodplain delineation and Draft FIRM Database. Plan for multiple additional grant years to reach effective FIRM.

## Cross-Cutting Observations

### 1. Mississippi River Corridor — Three Co-Occurring PMR Projects (21-02, 21-03, 21-04)
Rock Island, Whiteside, and Henry Counties are all advancing Mississippi River-adjacent PMRs using data from the same prior grant (MAS ISWS 17-06). These three projects share the same FEMA RPO, same period of performance, and all stop at QR1 FIRM Database. They should be coordinated for a unified Preliminary Map Production cycle to maximize efficiency and reduce community confusion. Whiteside and Henry are lower-budget ($150K and $125K) compared to Rock Island ($770K), reflecting Rock Island's more complex technical scope (new H&H, 2D modeling, levee analysis).

### 2. Multi-Year Data Development Pattern — Projects Needing Future Phases
Five projects are explicitly "data development only" with no pathway to final FIRM maps within this grant:
- **ISWS 21-06 (Montgomery)** — needs delineation, FIRM database, Preliminary, Post-Preliminary
- **ISWS 21-07 (Hamilton)** — same lifecycle gap
- **ISWS 21-08 (Wabash)** — explicitly flags "will request funding for QR-3 to Effective"
- **ISWS 21-12 (Kishwaukee)** — Phase 2 of data development; delineation and FIRM database still needed

These represent a significant budget investment in FFY21 ($1.7M combined) that will not yield final FIRM products without continued future funding. Planning ahead for FY23/FY25 MASs for these projects is important.

### 3. Levee Issues as a Cross-Cutting Bottleneck
Five projects involve levee complications that could delay final FIRM issuance:
- **Rock Island (21-02)** — 7 levee areas, PAL status pending H&H outcome
- **Wabash (21-08)** — cross-state levee modeling; "new floodway will have significant impact"
- **Peoria (21-09)** — ongoing multi-year levee engagement (3rd supplement MAS)
- **Bond (21-10)** — Keyesport Levee accreditation outcome controls mapping for entire community
- **Hamilton (21-07)** — levee identification ongoing

Levee accreditation timelines (USACE/FEMA) are often outside ISWS's control and represent the primary schedule risk across the portfolio.

### 4. Recurring Watersheds Suggest a Multi-Cycle Program
Several watersheds appear in prior-year MASs that are being carried forward:
- **ISWS 17-06** feeds Rock Island, Whiteside, and Henry PMRs in FY21
- **ISWS 18-03** continues into Wabash FY21
- **ISWS 18-04 / 20-13** continues into Peoria FY21 (3rd supplement)
- **ISWS 19-03** is completed by Stark FY21
- **ISWS 19-07** is supplemented by Bond FY21
- **ISWS 20-12** (Phase 1) is continued by Kishwaukee Phase 2 FY21

This demonstrates a 4–8+ year lifecycle from initial grant to effective FIRM for most studies, consistent with FEMA's Risk MAP program expectations.

### 5. Stark County (21-05) — Lone Project Reaching Final FIRM
Of all 9 FRP MASs, only **Stark County (ISWS 21-05)** is scoped to reach an effective FIRM within this grant period. This project completes the full lifecycle (Preliminary → Distribution → CCO → Due Process → Effective) and should be tracked as the portfolio's near-term "completion" project.

### 6. Kishwaukee — Largest Single FRP ($750K) and Most Complex Future Need
The Kishwaukee HUC8 Phase 2 is the largest single FRP investment at $750,000 and covers a major urban/suburban watershed (Rockford metro, McHenry/Kane collar counties). Given the population served, the complexity of the hydraulic analysis, and the certain need for 2+ additional future MASs, this project should receive attention in long-range grant planning.
