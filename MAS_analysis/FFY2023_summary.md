# FFY2023 MAS Portfolio Summary

## Overview

The FFY2023 portfolio for ISWS under the FEMA Cooperating Technical Partners (CTP) program contains **14 active projects** (15 files — one is a superseded version of ISWS 23-01 marked "do not use"). Projects span the full Risk MAP lifecycle from early-stage survey data acquisition through near-final preliminary FIRM production. The portfolio includes 2 programmatic SOWs (PM and COMS), 5 regulatory mapping/production projects (DFIRM/PMR through preliminary issuance), 5 data development projects, 1 hydraulics-only project, 1 outreach-coordination project, and 1 standalone survey. Total FEMA funding committed is approximately **$4.91M** (excluding the amendment ISWS 23-02A, which carries no additional funds). The dominant themes are: (1) bringing previously funded data development efforts into preliminary FIRM production across central and southern Illinois counties, (2) advancing Phase 4 of the large multi-phase Kishwaukee watershed study, and (3) building the data pipeline for future regulatory mapping along the Embarras and Kaskaskia watersheds. Nearly every production MAS stops at preliminary issuance and requires a future-funded phase for Due Process and final FIRM.

> **Note on files:** `ISWS_23-01-PM_SOW_POP_incorrect_dont_use.pdf` is a superseded version of the PM SOW with an incorrect Period of Performance. The corrected version (`ISWS_23-01-PM_SOW - POP_Fixed_by_GNH.pdf`) is the authoritative document.

### ISWS 23-01: Program Management (Statewide Illinois)
- **Activity Type:** Program Management (PM)
- **Phase:** Ongoing programmatic — not tied to a single mapping project phase; supports all other ISWS CTP projects

- **Multi-year:** Yes — PM is recurring; this SOW explicitly supports all companion flood risk project MAS across the full lifecycle (Discovery → Production → Preliminary → LFD). If companion COMS SOW (23-02) is not funded, Global Outreach tasks would need to be absorbed here.
- **Recommended Next Steps:** Ensure Business Plan is submitted within 4 months of award (by ~Jan 2024). CNMS deliverables must be submitted at each lifecycle milestone for all companion FY23 projects. Renew PM SOW for FFY2024+.

## Cross-Cutting Observations

### 1. All Production MAS Projects Stop at Preliminary — Due Process Phase Is a Funding Gap
Five regulatory mapping/production projects (ISWS 23-03 through 23-07) all explicitly exclude Due Process and Final Map Production tasks. This means that after completing Open Houses and CCO meetings, these projects will stall without a follow-on funded MAS to complete the regulatory cycle (appeal period resolution, LFD issuance, final FIRM printing). **All five need follow-on FFY2024 or FFY2025 MAS funding for Due Process + Final Map Production.** McDonough County (23-05, already at QR2) is likely the nearest-term candidate.

### 2. Edwards County and Wabash County Must Be Managed in Tandem
ISWS 23-06 and ISWS 23-07 share a common risk: Bonpas Creek forms the Edwards/Wabash county boundary, and any substantial comment on that reach will affect both projects' budgets and schedules. These two projects should be funded, coordinated, and advanced through Due Process simultaneously.

### 3. The Kishwaukee Watershed Is the Single Largest Investment — and Still Far from Final FIRM
At $1.697M, ISWS 23-08 (Phase 4 Data Development) represents 35% of the entire FFY2023 portfolio budget. The Kishwaukee multi-phase effort spans DeKalb, McHenry, Winnebago, Boone, and Ogle Counties. Phase 4 only reaches KDP2 (data development). Phase 5 (Preliminary production) and eventually Phase 6 (Due Process/Final FIRM) still need to be scoped and funded. The 3-county Phase 4 footprint is large — schedule Phase 5 carefully.

### 4. The Embarras River and Kaskaskia Are Long-Runway Pipeline Projects
ISWS 23-10 (Embarras River Hydraulics, $895K) is the hydraulic analysis step only; it follows survey/hydrology work from ISWS 22-07 and still requires floodplain mapping, Preliminary, and Final FIRM steps — plus tributary data development — before a Jasper County DFIRM can be produced. ISWS 23-14 (Kaskaskia Survey, $200K) is even earlier in the pipeline. Both are 4–6 year minimum pipelines from current state to final effective FIRM.

### 5. The Wabash River Corridor Appears Repeatedly
Wabash-related projects appear across four distinct MAS entries: ISWS 23-07 (Wabash County PMR), ISWS 23-09 (Alexander/Pulaski — Lower Ohio/Cache, Wabash confluence area), ISWS 23-10 (Embarras River confluence with Wabash), and ISWS 23-11 (Middle/Lower Wabash bi-state outreach). The Mt. Carmel levee (Wabash County) and Cairo levee system (Alexander County) both have unresolved accreditation/mapping paths that represent scheduling risk for advancing these projects.

### 6. Green River / Bureau County Dependency Appears Twice
Both ISWS 23-12 (Henry County) and ISWS 23-13 (Whiteside County) reference the Bureau County FY20 upstream model as a dependency. These three projects collectively form a connected Green River data pipeline. Consider sequencing or bundling a future PMR/DFIRM MAS that covers all three counties' new data in a single production phase.

### 7. Kaskaskia Watershed Has Two Simultaneous Touchpoints
ISWS 23-02 (COMS) focuses on Kaskaskia strategic planning and community prioritization for future mapping, while ISWS 23-14 launches the survey data pipeline for Kaskaskia H&H development. Coordinating the prioritization output from 23-02 with the target stream selection for 23-14 and future H&H MAS is essential to ensure survey data collected matches where modeling effort will ultimately go.

### 8. Budget Concentration Risk
Three projects (ISWS 23-08 Kishwaukee: $1.697M; ISWS 23-10 Embarras: $895K; ISWS 23-09 Alexander/Pulaski: $395K) account for approximately 61% of total FFY2023 project funding. These are all data development projects that have not yet produced a regulatory product. Performance risk management is important for these three.

### 9. All Projects Have Zero Partner Leverage
Every project in the FFY2023 portfolio lists $0 partner contribution/leverage. This is consistent with ISWS's CTP model but worth noting — there may be opportunities in future cycles to document in-kind contributions from IDNR-OWR or county partners.
